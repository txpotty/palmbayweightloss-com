    How Medical Weight Loss Works in Vero Beach South

    Medical weight loss treats excess weight as a chronic condition. Your provider looks for causes like insulin resistance, untreated sleep apnea, thyroid problems or medicines known to add weight before building a plan. Prescriptions are optional. When medication fits, semaglutide and tirzepatide are frequent picks because they reduce appetite.

    Who Medical Weight Loss Is For

    Adults who have not reached a healthy weight with diet and exercise alone are the usual fit. Prescription medicines are generally considered for a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with a weight-related condition like high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol or sleep apnea. Coaching works below those cutoffs. Medication is not used during pregnancy.

    Treatment Schedule in Vero Beach South

    Week one is the intake and review. A weekly injection plan steps up every four weeks, while a daily pill plan usually means one tablet each morning, sometimes building over a month. Early follow-ups are frequent. Once you are stable, check-ins usually space out to every one to three months.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    The first two weeks focus on habits and, if prescribed, a starting dose. Many people on GLP-1 medicine notice less hunger within the first week or two, and mild stomach symptoms show up most after starting and after each dose increase. Progress is gradual. A loss of 1 to 2 pounds a week is a common target once a plan is underway, though medication plans vary.

    Side Effects in Vero Beach South

    Side effects depend on the treatment. Injections tend to cause stomach symptoms, and stimulant pills tend to cause dry mouth, insomnia and a quicker pulse. Fast loss raises gallstone risk. Protein at every meal and resistance exercise two or three times a week help protect muscle while you lose weight.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening covers your BMI, conditions, medications and weight history. Certain medicines are ruled out by a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, uncontrolled high blood pressure, pregnancy or a history of eating disorders. Recent bloodwork helps. Common asks are A1C, a lipid panel, thyroid tests and a metabolic panel from the past year.

    Medical Weight Loss Compared With Other Approaches in Vero Beach South

    Commercial diet programs cannot prescribe. A medical program covers the same food and habit work but adds a provider who can prescribe, order labs and change medicines that contribute to weight gain. Within medical care, options range from FDA-approved Wegovy and Zepbound to daily pills and compounded alternatives, which are not FDA-approved. Bariatric surgery fits some higher BMIs.
